PART 3: CAN VEV_4000 BE IMPROVED?
========================================================================

The current strategy captures ~$2,948/day. Let's decompose this.

SPREAD CAPTURE ANALYSIS:
  With ~155 trades/day, ~77 per side (balanced), and our spread of 19:
  Each buy at intrinsic-9.4, each sell at intrinsic+9.4.
  If buys and sells alternate perfectly: 77 round-trips * 18.8 = $1,448/day.
  The remaining ~$1,500/day comes from INVENTORY MTM:
  With delta=1.0, any net long inventory gains on VFE up-days.
  Day 2 (VFE +28): MTM on avg net position of ~50 → 50*28 = $1,400.
  This matches the data.

COULD WE WIDEN THE QUOTE?
  Current: bb+1 / ba-1 (1 tick inside bot each side).
  At bb / ba (same as bot): share fills pro-rata 30/(30+11) = 73%.
    77 * 0.73 = 56 RTs. 56 * 20.8 = $1,165. WORSE (fewer fills).
  At bb+2 / ba-2 (2 ticks inside): still all flow to us.
    77 RTs * 16.8 = $1,294. WORSE (less capture per RT).

  VERDICT: bb+1/ba-1 is already the OPTIMAL quoting strategy.
  It maximizes the product of (fill_probability × spread_capture).
  1 tick inside the bot gives 100% priority at 18.8 per RT.

COULD WE INCREASE SIZE OR CAP?
  Current: size=30, cap=100.
  Taker trades 2-3 per trade. Size 30 already absorbs full trade. Size 50
  adds nothing — all taker volume is consumed at 30.
  Cap 100 is rarely hit (balanced flow → position oscillates around 0).
  Increasing cap to 200 only helps if position drifts past 100, which requires
  a sustained imbalance (~40+ ticks of one-sided flow). Rare but possible.

  CHANGE: Increase cap from 100 to 150. Low risk, marginal benefit.
  Expected: +$0-200/day on rare occasions.

COULD WE HEDGE DELTA?
  Delta=1.0, so our VEV_4000 position is equivalent to VFE position.
  Hedging: sell VFE against long VEV_4000 position.
  But VFE has spread ~5. Each hedge costs 2.5 ticks (half-spread).
  With ~77 buys/day needing hedges: 77 * 2.5 = $192.5 hedging cost.
  This REDUCES PnL. The MTM from inventory drift ($1,500/day) >> hedge cost,
  and since flow is balanced, the unhedged PnL volatility averages out.

  VERDICT: Do NOT hedge. The unhedged strategy is better.

VEV_4000 BOTTOM LINE:
  Current strategy is near-optimal. +$150-200/day from cap increase.
  No other changes warranted. This is a solved problem.

  From R1 ACO analysis: ~59 fills/2k ticks from inside-spread posting on a
  stable-FV product. Extrapolated to 10k: ~295 fills.
  VEV_4500 has spread=16 and our code posts at bb+1/ba-1 (inside by 1 tick).
  If the invisible taker mechanism works similarly: ~295 fills/day.
  Each fill at bb+1 (buy side) or ba-1 (sell side), capture ~14 per RT.
  With ~148 RTs: 148 * 14 = $2,072/day.

  BUT: this is WEBSITE-ONLY. The BT has no invisible taker model.
  AND: the R1 ACO comparison may not transfer (ACO is a fixed-FV product
  while VEV_4500 moves with VFE).
  AND: the R1 ACO fill rate was for a product with completely different
  bot parameters (spread=16 for ACO, also 16 for VEV_4500 — coincidence?).

WHAT CAN WE CHANGE IN THE CODE?
  1. Increase size from 30 to 50: more aggressive inside-spread posting

  2. Post at intrinsic ± 1 instead of bb+1/ba-1:

     This narrows our spread from 14 to 2, reducing capture per RT dramatically.
     But it provides MUCH better price to the invisible taker, potentially
     increasing fill rate. Net effect: uncertain.

RECOMMENDATION:

  The invisible taker fills are the only opportunity and they can't be

PART 5: CAN VEV_6000 / VEV_6500 BE EXPLOITED?
========================================================================

Current: SKIPPED entirely. PnL = $0.

THE OPPORTUNITY: "FREE OPTIONS" AT PRICE ZERO
  Book: bid=0, ask=1, mid=0.5. Constant forever.
  Taker: sells ~320-345 contracts/day at price 0.
  If we post a passive bid at 0, the taker hits our bid.
  We buy at 0. Cost basis = 0. Mark-to-mid = 0.5. Unrealized PnL = +0.5/contract.

HOW FILLS WORK:
  1. Bot posts bid=0, ask=1
  2. We post bid at 0, size S
  3. Our bid at 0 goes passive (doesn't cross ask at 1)
  4. Taker sells at bid=0, hitting both bot and our bid
  5. Pro-rata fill: our_share = S / (S + bot_L1)
     With S=30, bot_L1=22: share = 30/52 = 58%
     With S=100, bot_L1=22: share = 100/122 = 82%

EXPECTED FILLS PER DAY:
  ~95 taker events, average 3.5 contracts each.
  At S=30: 0.58 * 95 * 3.5 = ~193 contracts. But cap at 300.
  At S=100: 0.82 * 95 * 3.5 = ~273 contracts. Fills most of cap.

PNL CALCULATION:
  Buy at 0, mark at 0.5. Per contract: +$0.5.
  300 contracts * $0.5 = $150 per strike.
  Two strikes (6000 + 6500): $300 total.

  TIMING: accumulation takes ~30-50 taker events to fill 300 contracts.
  At ~1 event per 100 ticks, full cap reached by tick ~3,000-5,000.
  For the 1k-tick website test: fill ~100-150 contracts per strike.
  1k-tick PnL: ~100 * 0.5 * 2 = $100.

RISKS:
  1. SETTLEMENT: If the game settles at intrinsic (=0) rather than mid (=0.5),
     the realized PnL at settlement = 0 - 0 = $0. But the WITHIN-DAY PnL
     at the website test endpoint (tick 1000) uses mid, not intrinsic.
     So for the website test, this works. For the full 10k day, depends
     on whether end-of-day evaluation uses mid or settlement.

  2. BID AT ZERO: The game engine must accept Order(sym, 0, qty). Price=0
     is a valid integer for a worthless option. If the engine rejects
     price=0 orders, this strategy fails. NEEDS TESTING.

  3. POSITION LIMIT: posting large bids (100+) at 0 means total_buy = 100.
     Position starts at 0, so 0 + 100 = 100 <= 300. Fine. As position grows,
     we must reduce bid size: remaining = 300 - pos. Standard headroom logic.

  4. ADVERSE INTERACTION: VEV_6000/6500 are currently in VOUCHER_STRIKES_SKIP.
     The intrinsic arb layer skips them. The call-spread arb layer skips pairs
     involving them. No interference with other layers.

IMPLEMENTATION:
  - Remove 6000/6500 from VOUCHER_STRIKES_SKIP
  - Add new layer: for K in [6000, 6500], post passive bid at 0, size 50,
    cap 300. No asks (no buyer flow).
  - OR: keep them in SKIP for all other layers, add a dedicated "free OTM"
    layer that ONLY posts passive bids at 0.

IS THIS WORTH IT?
  $150/strike = $300 total for both. On 1k ticks: ~$100.
  It's free money with zero risk (cost=0, can't lose more than you paid).
  Implementation: ~10 lines of code. Trivially simple.

  VERDICT: YES, implement. Small but free.

PART 7: NO THETA DECAY IN THIS GAME
========================================================================

Critical finding: time value is CONSTANT within each day.

  VEV_4000/4500 time value: 0.01 mean, 0.82 std, NO quintile trend.
  VEV_6000/6500 time value: 0.50 constant, ZERO variance.

The options do NOT decay like real options. The bot maintains constant spreads
and quote levels regardless of TTE. The only price movement comes from VFE
spot movement (delta exposure), not from theta decay.

This has two implications:
  1. "Theta carry" as a strategy name is misleading — there IS no theta.
     The VEV_4000 MM strategy profits from SPREAD CAPTURE, not theta.
  2. Buying OTM options doesn't have a theta drag within the trading day.
     The 1k-tick website test (~100 seconds) sees zero theta erosion.
     Buy-and-hold OTM options is less costly than it would be in real markets.

BETWEEN days, TTE decreases by 1 day (5→4→3). The cross-day effect on
pricing is embedded in the bot's quote levels, which appear stable. We
don't observe cross-day data within a single submission, so this is moot.

PART 9: RISK ASSESSMENT — WHY VEV_4000 MIGHT LOSE MONEY
========================================================================

VEV_4000 earns $2,437-3,367/day, but this INCLUDES inventory MTM.

  Day 0 (VFE -6):   $3,039 — despite VFE drop, spread capture dominates
  Day 1 (VFE +20):  $3,367 — spread capture + long MTM
  Day 2 (VFE +28):  $2,437 — paradoxically LESS than day 0

Why day 2 is lower despite VFE going up more:
  Fewer trades on day 2 (128 vs 172 on day 0). Less spread capture.
  The MTM from delta exposure is a BONUS, not the core edge.
  Core edge = bb+1/ba-1 spread capture of ~18.8 per round-trip.

STRESS SCENARIO: VFE drops 50 points in a day (never seen in data but possible).
  Net long inventory of ~50 → MTM loss = 50 * 50 = -$2,500.
  Spread capture of ~$1,400 partially offsets.
  Net: -$1,100 on a bad day. SURVIVABLE.

This is why increasing the cap BEYOND 150 is risky — larger inventory
amplifies the MTM swing. 150 is a reasonable balance.
